Post Nature

Art DirectionUX/UIResearchAI DesignConceptVisual IdentityExhibition

Info

Post Nature is a speculative interactive exhibition exploring human intervention in the plant world. Through a physical installation and two digital interfaces, it invites visitors to examine how plants have changed over time - and imagine what we might turn them into next.

Role

Graduation Project, HIT
Mentor: Bob Orel

Year

2025

For millennia, humans have cultivated and selectively bred plants to meet practical and aesthetic needs. Today, genetic engineering and DNA editing make that intervention more precise than ever.

Post-Nature questions the logic behind these changes: what we choose to preserve, improve, or eliminate - and what we might lose when nature is redesigned around human desires.

The exhibition was designed as a pristine, almost clinical environment, balancing delicate beauty with a subtle sense of unease. At its entrance, four transparent acrylic panels display speculative plant specimens as objects of observation and control. Two light-toned archive screens trace the history of human intervention in plants and imagine where it might lead in the future, while a contrasting dark kiosk invites visitors to create a hybrid of their own.

The archive traces ten plant varieties across seven stages of transformation. The first five are grounded in historical and scientific research, while the final two imagine possible futures. Moving through each plant’s timeline reveals changes in its appearance and characteristics, making the cumulative effects of human intervention visible.

Each profile brings together the plant’s identity, history, visual transformation and stage-specific information in a single view.

Visitors combine two speculative plants and shape their hybrid through choices about appearance, functionality, and unique traits. Every enhancement comes with a warning about its potential ecological cost, even as the experience encourages further modification. As the hybrid becomes increasingly artificial, customization becomes a test of how far we are willing to go to improve nature. At the end, visitors can access their creation on their phones, alongside a record of their choices and their possible environmental consequences.

The central UX challenge was to turn a complex decision system into a clear, intuitive experience without losing its critical dimension. The flow was divided into short, optional stages where visitors can preview each modification and decide whether to continue or finalize their hybrid. This structure gives visitors control over the process while gradually revealing the consequences of their choices.

Extensive botanical, historical, and scientific research formed the foundation of Post Nature, organized through research maps and a structured content database. This foundation informed the interactive experiences and guided a broad process of AI-assisted visual exploration, art direction, and image curation, shaping the visual evolution of each plant.
